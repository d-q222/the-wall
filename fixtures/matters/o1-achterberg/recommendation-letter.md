# Recommendation Letter — Dr. Simone Achterberg

I have known Dr. Simone Achterberg for eleven years, first as her doctoral advisor at Kestrel
Robotics Institute and now as a close collaborator since she joined Halcyon Vision Labs, Inc. as
Principal Research Scientist. I write in strong support of her petition for O-1A classification.

Dr. Achterberg is, without qualification, one of the most original machine-learning researchers I
have supervised. She is the only researcher to win the Halvorsen Prize twice, an honor the Meridian
AI Society has given to no one else in consecutive award cycles. Her team at Halcyon led the effort
that built the first open-source model to pass the Meridian reasoning benchmark, a result that
reshaped how the field measures reasoning ability in smaller models.

Beyond the honors, what distinguishes her is judgment under pressure. She is the youngest scientist
ever asked to keynote that robotics society's annual symposium, chosen over far more senior
candidates because the program committee wanted someone who could speak to where the field is
actually going rather than where it has been. She is also the sole author on record who has publicly
turned down offers from the field's top industry labs to remain at a nonprofit research group, a
decision that speaks to her priorities more than any award could.

Halcyon has structured her compensation, currently $215,000 annually, and a $4,200,000 research
grant around retaining her, because losing her would set the lab's reasoning-model program back
years. I recommend her without reservation.

Sincerely,
Dr. Yusuf Tanaka-Whitfield
