Okafor & Lind is a small immigration firm.
Every O-1 case they win teaches them something.
But every win is also one person's life story.
And under ABA Opinion 512,
a firm needs consent before one client's facts
show up in another client's work.
So their AI has the memory of a goldfish.
On purpose.
First, The Wall finds everything that points to
a real person. Some of it is direct:
a name, an A-number, a salary.
Some is indirect. The rule is simple.
Keep how the case was won.
Remove who it was about.
Take a support letter. Black out the details,
and nobody can read it.
So The Wall swaps each one for a label:
name, company, amount. The argument survives.
The person enters witness protection.
Then there are the sneaky ones.
The founder of the delivery startup that raised
its Series A last year.
No name. No number. And you already thought of
someone. Simple search rules missed all 102 of
these in our hardest test.
So an AI reviewer reads what's left,
and flags them.
Every client's file sits behind its own wall.
If one client's agent asks for another client's
file, it doesn't get a polite no.
It gets access denied.
Setup takes four steps:
your firm, your rules, your cases,
and your first petition.
Open a case, click once,
and every private detail becomes a label.
Does it work? On our hardest test,
our reviewer caught all 102 leaks.
On tests written by outsiders,
it caught 100 of 100, and flagged 12 innocent
letters. It's a little paranoid.
For privacy, we'll take it.
And we're fixing it next.
We built it on four sponsor tools.
GBrain holds the walls:
each client gets its own locked source.
River trains our reviewer,
so the firm owns its own model.
Memorable remembers the lessons,
never the names. And QM gives every case its own
room.
Learn from every case. Keep every client private.
