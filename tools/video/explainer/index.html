Meet Okafor & Lind, a small immigration firm.
They've won a lot of O-1 visa cases.
Each win is a great example to learn from.
But each one is also one person's private file.
Today: how we take out private details,
catch the sneaky ones, and lock each client's
files away.
First, we find the details that point to a real
person: the name, the ID numbers,
the employer, the salary.
Some are easy to spot. Some are not.
The rule is simple. Keep how the case was won.
Remove who it was about.
Take a support letter. We could black out each
detail, but then you can't tell what was there.
Instead, The Wall swaps each one for a label,
like name, company, or amount.
The letter still reads clearly.
Some details are sneaky.
This sentence has no name and no number,
but it still points to one person.
Simple search rules missed all 102 of these in
our hardest test. So an AI reviewer reads what's
left, and flags them.
Each client's files sit behind their own wall.
When one client's assistant asks for another client's
file, the answer is always the same:
access denied. It's a real lock,
not a polite request.
Here's the app. A firm sets up in four quick steps:
the firm, its rules, its cases,
and a first petition. Then open a case and click
one button. Every private detail turns into a
label, and the AI reviewer checks what's left.
Here are our honest results.
On our hardest test, our AI reviewer caught all
102 hidden leaks. On new tests written by others,
it caught all 100, but it also raised 12 false
alarms on 100 clean letters.
That's what we're fixing next.
Learn from every case. Keep every client private.
That's The Wall.
